# ASD-STE100 reference notes

Distilled structure of the specification, for when a rule citation is needed. The spec itself is a paid standard; these are the publicly documented rules. Nothing here is a substitute for the spec — it is a citation sheet.

## Scale of the standard

- Current edition published January 2025: 53 writing rules plus a dictionary of approximately 900 approved words.
- Roughly 1,200 non-approved words carry suggested replacements.
- Each approved word is permitted at exactly one part of speech and one meaning.
- Organizations may define their own approved technical nouns and verbs on top of the base ~900 for domain vocabulary.

## Rule groups worth citing

Section 1 — Words:
1.1 use approved words, technical nouns, technical verbs; 1.2 approved words only as the specified part of speech; 1.3 approved meanings only; 1.4 only approved forms of verbs and adjectives; 1.6 a non-approved word only when it is a technical noun or part of one; 1.7 do not use technical nouns as verbs; 1.9 when selecting a technical noun prefer short and easy to understand; 1.10 no regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns; 1.11 do not use different technical nouns for the same item; 1.13 do not use technical verbs as nouns; 1.14 American English spelling unless directed otherwise.

Section 4 — Sentences: 4.1 short and clear sentences; 4.2 do not omit words or use contractions to make sentences shorter; 4.3 use a vertical list for complex texts.

Section 5 — Procedures: verbs in the imperative form; one instruction per sentence; a procedure sentence is at most about 20 words.

Section 6 — Descriptive writing: 6.1 give information gradually; 6.2 use key words and key phrases to give the text logical structure; 6.3 a maximum of 25 words per sentence; 6.6 no paragraph has more than six sentences.

## Vocabulary rule

Approved words are listed in uppercase in the spec. APPROVED (uppercase) words are allowed; lowercase entries are not approved and have suggested alternatives. Quote this distinction when a model asks what "approved" means.

## Worked shape (from the spec's own example types)

The spec rewrites long descriptive sentences by splitting them into short ones rather than cutting information. A 31-word sentence becomes two sentences of 15 and 16 words. When relaxing to "80% of the way to STE", this splitting is the rule worth keeping — it is what carries most of the readability gain.

## Limitations

- STE is an English controlled language; it does not transfer directly to Chinese output.
- The dictionary is copyrighted; do not reproduce word lists into a skill or prompt.
- Adoption in industry is partial, and misapplication (treating it as word-count policing rather than vocabulary control) is a documented failure mode.
